4. Executive Stress-Test: Key Board Questions & Tradeoffs
Candid answers to the three toughest objections before presenting to unit owners
Q1: We just raised common charges +9% in 2026 and levied a $2.5M assessment—why not draw down our $1.36M reserve to bridge 2027?
We already do in this model. The simulator draws down the entire $355,062 cushion above our $1.00M board-mandated rainy-day floor in 2027 before levying a single dollar of new assessment. Drawing below $1.00M would expose a 249-unit high-rise with an aging heating plant to emergency credit risk and lender scrutiny.
Q2: With $631k budgeted for #2 heating oil in 2027, why commit $516k to a boiler gas conversion in 2028?
It is our primary lever to stop future operating spikes. Converting to dual-fuel natural gas cuts an estimated ~$50,000/yr in winter heating costs (~10-year simple payback) while directly addressing NYC Local Law 97 carbon penalties ($36,696/yr starting 2030). Funding it from reserves avoids another special assessment.
Q3: Does a higher monthly common charge hurt apartment resale values?
Balance matters. An oversized +25% one-year jump creates sticker shock on listings; conversely, back-to-back multi-million-dollar assessments trigger Fannie Mae/Freddie Mac warrantability reviews and scare financed buyers. A measured +6.0% reserve line (especially paired with staging Tier 2 discretionary items like the $325k generator) satisfies lender reserve rules while keeping monthly charges competitive.
